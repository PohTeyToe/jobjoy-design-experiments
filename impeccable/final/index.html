Orientation
Section I

How to read this report.

Read once through without a pen. Return, on a second or third pass, to the passages that arrest you — yours is the final authority on what rings true.

Each of us is born with natural talents and abilities. When we honour and make use of these talents, we feel good about ourselves. When we do not, we feel unfulfilled. By identifying what motivates us, what our talents are, and how to employ them, we can target work environments and careers with which we are compatible and subsequently experience “JobJoy.”

Your JobJoy Report, which identifies your Key Success Factors, is an excellent tool to enable you to achieve a deep sense of job satisfaction in your life. It starts with a definition of your Natural Talents, then describes the elements that trigger your talents: Preferred Subject Matter, Motivating Situations, Natural Relating Style. These four facets come together to serve your Essential Motivation. See your Individual Passion Pattern (IPP). This is the essence of what you do in terms of your right work when you are doing what you enjoy most and doing it well.

Using the facts, people, and events of your stories, I build a case toward these conclusions. I provide a Personal Statement in which I re-tell your story with the view of painting a more accurate and reliable word picture of who you are in terms of your right work. The numbers in your Personal Statement correspond with numbers on your Definitions pages. This allows us to build a strong vocabulary to communicate to potential employers the job duties and conditions that make you a motivated, productive employee. Finally, I provide suggested Career Options that align with your IPP and outline strategies to move into a better jobfit.

On reading your report, you may find some things resonate well while others do not. Make note of these and send any comments to me. Often, people obtain the best use of their JobJoy Report after reading it several times to understand the true impact of its revelations. Careful reflection and validation of its contents can take time. Many people refer to their Report often, using it for clarification and inspiration. Enjoy your Report.

Definitions i — vi

Read these once, then return to them after the statement.

Definition markers are set in lowercase Roman numerals so that arabic numerals in the prose are reserved for dates and quantities.

  1. Natural Talents

    What do you tend to do naturally and easily, that you find yourself doing almost without thinking or planning to do it, and that you always enjoy? You could call these your natural talents. There are many different kinds of natural talents that people have related to learning, evaluating, organizing, planning, communicating, influencing, observing, doing, developing, and more. When you use your natural talents as the basis for your job, then your job becomes a more enjoyable part of your life.

  2. Preferred Subject Matter

    What and whom do you enjoy working with the most? Your Preferred Subject Matter is the combination of people, information, and modalities that you are most happy to deal with and which motivates you to do your best.

  3. Motivating Situations

    The situations that turn you into a motivated person, or the conditions that stimulate you to achieve, including: the circumstances that trigger your involvement, how the activity is structured, what job circumstances stimulate you, what kind of recognition motivates you, what is the end use or result.

  4. Natural Relating Style

    The way in which you prefer to relate to others as you use your Natural Talents to accomplish your Essential Motivation — your most comfortable way to relate with other people in terms of Personality Style, Relationship Style, Relationship Type, natural Role, and how you prefer to be Managed.

  5. Essential Motivation

    The singular, distinguishing, or characteristic result that drives all the other parts of your pattern. It is the central purpose you seek to achieve.

  6. What You Do Next

    These are the things you must do to create the unique work that is yours. Do what you love. The money will follow.

Personal Statement.

I am : A builder & developer of creative projects.

I like to put together the various parts of a creative project. (1) And I love developing skills that facilitate my ability to build creative projects. This natural inclination showed up early in life.

I remember having a very vivid imagination and could entertain myself for hours with drawing or building forts. I could draw dinosaurs and played Robin Hood and cowboys. I enjoyed using my hands and imagination to create. (2) For example, I built with Lego and Kenex as a kid. I usually would follow the directions in the Lego box to build the design on the box. I found it more enjoyable to follow the instruction with Lego but would build creatively whatever I wanted with Kenex. (3) I have enjoyed building with Lego and Kenex as an adult but less as a regular hobby and more of a nostalgic good time — but I still get a kick out of seeing a finished product result from my creative efforts.

Personal Statement
Continuation

I’ve always been drawn to visual material, including colours, design, and drawing. For example, in grade 3 I wrote a book about a travelling bear for fun and drew all the pictures (had recently learned about author-illustrators). My teacher was so impressed she made photocopies and had it laminated and bound so that future kids in her class could read it. In grade 5, we had a project to paint a picture of a building. My painting reflected a talent and was chosen to be displayed with an extremely select few students from the school district, hung in the mayoral office of Victoria. Grade 12 was the first and only time I ever took an art class and I made two distinct pieces that were particularly good. (4) I enjoy subject matter that is visual in nature, including the dynamics of form, line, colour, and contrast.

But I was especially drawn to movies. Amazingly, I made my first movie at 6 or 7 y.o. with my dad behind the camera because I was too young to operate it. I told him what to shoot and how to narrate it. It was about a dinosaur attacking a city and we used toys to make it. Equally amazing, I made my second movie at 8 y.o., “The Wild Adventures of Sheriff Jane,” starring my 6 y.o. sister, my younger cousins, my dad, aunt, and grandparents. My grandma entered it into a local film festival for fun when I was 12 y.o. and it won audience favourite.

I didn’t have a script but I knew how I wanted the story to go; I knew what every scene should be or look like before we shot it. I made all the props like a stagecoach and bank teller bars. (5) I got my family to dress up in their “costumes” and would block out the scene and tell them what to say before I rolled the camera. We shot it in scene order so we wouldn’t have to edit it. I liked seeing my vision come to life. (6)

This was me in action. I love to explore and try new things which go beyond the current limits of my knowledge or experience. It usually leads to some kind of vision or goal for something I want to build or develop. It could be a big thing like a movie or a small thing like a painting easel. But, when I have a vision for a project, I can approach it from multiple angles then jump right in and actually get my hands on to whatever objects or materials or processes I need to make my vision or goal into a finished product.

I went on to make many small films through my teens. In grade 9, I discovered that the Victoria Film Festival had a youth category and I would make at least three short films that would show in the festival over the course of my high school years. I would do most of the creative and production work, including editing, which was my favourite activity. (7) I have a knack for developing my creative projects by giving the thing its shape or form.

These early years of my life reveal so much about my motivational pattern. I thrive in an open-ended environment that inspires and encourages creativity, where people focus their energy or efforts on producing distinctive and unique objects or ideas. (8) I respond to a clearly defined or predetermined end, whether the goal is set by me or others.

Continuation

I loved making people laugh. I loved the attention and later the praise that came with being funny or clever. I enjoyed being funny “on the fly.” (9) I liked being in front of an audience. I think my natural personality is a performer. I like to make speeches, be on stage, or give presentations of any kind. I like to influence and motivate others.

Although I performed in many of my high schools’ musicals and plays from grades 10–12, my favourite drama project was The Laramie Project in Grade 11. It felt fun to build something with a group, to run lines, to take directors’ notes, to make it as good as we could. I remember performing and how it felt to be in front of the audience, working together with your classmates so closely. (10) I really enjoy working with and through groups of people.

My mom put my sister and I in camp when we were 9 and 11. I found a community there that would keep me going back until I was old enough to work there. (11) I do best when I’m in an environment where there is some freedom to talk and interact and make new friends. But also the outdoors. (12) Because I like to do things physically and manually, the outdoors makes it easier to use my physical abilities because it requires them.

The camp experience allowed me to test my mettle even as a young kid. I liked feeling proficient at survival type activities, like making shelters for overnights and building fires, orienteering, feeling physically challenged. (13) I love to develop my knowledge or skill to the point of proficiency then demonstrate my competency or capability by solving problems, producing results, or advising others.

The summer I was 16, we went on a 25-day out-trip where we started kayaking the Gulf Islands, canoeing the Campbell Lake system, and hiking to the peak of Mt. Elkhorn. We had to rely on each other and often were with each other in very deep and desperate moments, navigating not just the land but our own emotional teenage landscapes. (14) I function best within the context of a group, team, or organization that I identify with, or shares my values or enthusiasm for a certain experience.

I really do like to explore and break through the existing limits of my experience and travelling allows for that. Driving to Arizona and back in less than a week with my best friend at 23 in 2012 was a highlight for me. It felt like a big adventure, seeing places and things we’d never seen before. It felt like freedom. (15)

Maybe I’m usually happiest going solo, completely in charge, making all the decisions. I find myself resisting outside influence, and do not feel comfortable on teams or in partnerships unless I can maintain personal autonomy. I liked the sense of adventure and felt brave to be alone, like I was tough and capable. (16) As a creative person, I am resourceful and self-contained and find that creativity increases with freedom.

Section V · Definitions
Definitions

Of your Key Success Factors.

These definitions correspond with the (1)–(16) markers in your Personal Statement. The selections shown are illustrative; your final report draws each from George’s GLOSSARY based on your stories.
  1. (1)Building — Putting together the various parts of a project so that the parts make a unified whole.
  2. (2)By Imagining — Forming a mental picture of something not yet present, then giving the picture a shape you can build toward.
  3. (3)Tangibles, Physical, Manual — Working with concrete objects you can shape with your hands.
  4. (4)Sensory: Visual — Subject matter that engages the eye: form, line, colour, contrast.
  5. (5)By Designing — Devising the arrangement of parts before building, so the build follows a clear plan.
  6. (6)End Result, Finished Product — Working toward a completed object you can point to and say “I made that.”
  7. (7)Developing — Taking a starting point and giving it shape, refinement, or finish until it is ready for its audience.
  8. (8)Goal-Oriented — Responding to a clearly defined predetermined end, whether the goal is set by you or others.
  9. (9)Performer — Engaging an audience with planned material delivered live; recognition is part of the work.
  10. (10)Group, Team — Doing your best work with others around you who share the task.
  11. (11)Social — Environments where freedom to talk and meet new people is part of the day.
  12. (12)Outdoors, Nature — Working in physical, open environments rather than enclosed indoor settings.
  13. (13)Proficiency — Developing knowledge or skill to a level where you can demonstrate it on demand.
  14. (14)Identifying with Group — Functioning best inside a group, team, or organization whose values you share.
  15. (15)Travel, Move Around — Work that takes you to new places rather than keeping you in one location.
  16. (16)Solo, Autonomous — Resourceful and self-contained; creativity increases with personal freedom.

Career Options

Suggested directions.

These suggestions align with your Individual Passion Pattern. Each is a starting point for further exploration, not a prescription.

Suggested Career Options.

Independent Filmmaker / Documentary Producer

Work that lets you carry a project from imagination to a finished film, with your hands on the craft from script to edit. Fits your pull toward visual material, your goal-oriented streak, and your preference for owning the build.

Day-to-day: blocking out scenes and props before a shoot; cutting a sequence in the edit suite until the rhythm reads.

Industrial / Product Designer

A studio practice where you sketch, prototype, and refine tangible objects until they hold their final form. The work rewards designing before building, the visual eye, and the satisfaction of a finished product you can hand someone.

Day-to-day: sketching iterations of a chair leg or tool grip; building a foam mock-up to test how it sits in the hand.

Creative-Project Lead in a Small Studio

Leading a small group through a creative build — campaign, exhibit, short film — where you set the vision and stay close enough to the making to keep your hands on the work. Suits your performer streak, your taste for groups whose values you share, and your need for autonomy at the centre.

Day-to-day: walking a team through the day’s shot list; jumping into the edit when a scene isn’t landing.
